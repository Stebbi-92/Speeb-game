# Building

_This page is made from the game's own data by `tools/wiki.gd`. Edit the game, not this page._

Press **B** to build. Scroll to choose a piece, left click to place it, **R** to turn it, right click to take a built piece down (you get most of it back). A campfire makes a base: chests within it are used when you make or build there.

While building, the pack key (**I**) opens the pieces to pick from. Rain and snow stop under a roof, and a sealed base keeps a storm's haze out.

## What opens when

A new world builds only a camp. Each fire lit opens more, for the whole fellowship, and the later pieces want the stuff of the harder lands. Pieces not yet open show greyed in the build menu with what opens them.

| Opens with | Pieces |
|---|---|
| The start | Campfire, Torch, Bedroll, Storage Chest, Workbench, Floor, Timber Wall, Doorway, Door, Roof, Spike Barricade |
| Light the first fire | Cooking Station, Canvas Tent, Barrel, Window Wall, Stairs, Stone Wall, Table, Chair, Rug |
| Light the second fire | Bed, Shelf, Oil Lamp, Standing Lamp, Iron Stove, Palisade, Iron Brazier, Strongbox, Watchtower |
| Light the third fire | Planter Box, Anvil, Fellowship Banner, Iron-bound Barricade |
| Light the fourth fire | Waystone, Tar Lamp-post |
| Fell a great one | Trophy Shrine |
| Fell 3 great ones | Trophy Hall |

## Building up

Look at a wall, window, doorway or floor and press **E** to build it up a step.

## Settlers

A snug base (a lit fire, walls, a roof, a light) with a spare bed draws a wanderer at dawn now and then, up to three a fire. Each keeps to a trade and leaves what it brings in the base's chest each dawn.

- The first of each trade to settle teaches the fellowship a recipe: the woodcutter the Shipwright's Folio, the salt-digger the Salter's Receipts, the scavenger the Gunwright's Ledger, the hunter the Herbwife's Notes, and the fire-keeper the Lamplighter's Primer.
- Each asks for something now and then (What now says what, when you stand at the base). Leave it in the base's chest: at dawn it is taken, and they leave something good in its place.
- Let the fire die and they go.
