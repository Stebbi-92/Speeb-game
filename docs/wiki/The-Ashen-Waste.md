# The Ashen Waste

An open world, about four kilometres across, made fresh from a seed. Each world keeps its own save, and up to eight can walk it together.

The world is laid out as you move: the ground near you is in full detail, the far ground in less (this makes towns much smoother). While a world loads, a bar shows how far it has got, with changing hints.

## What the world shows you, and when

A new world starts simple. Each system shows up, with a short card, the first time the world needs it: **fear** at the first night, **hardships** and their wards at the first hard land, **trades** at the first town, **road safety** when a town first pleads for safer roads, and **seasons** from day five. The world keeps what it has shown, so anyone who joins later sees all of it.

## World settings

Each world has its own settings, chosen next to **Begin** and changed by the host from the pause menu: difficulty, enemy skill, enemy numbers, resources, hunger and tiredness, air support, unlimited ammunition, night pace, and whether you drop your pack when you fall.

**Night pace** sets how hard the nights press. Steady brings smaller rounds with longer breaks and fewer named nights. Chaos brings bigger rounds with shorter breaks, and most nights are named.

**Vows** are sworn only when a world begins, and kept for its whole life. Each makes the road harder and pays for it:
- **Vow of Glass:** you take half again as much harm, but every weapon learns twice as fast.
- **Vow of the Hunted:** every night from the third is a named night, and their dawn gifts are doubled.
- **Vow of the Tithe:** traders pay half for what you sell, but each lit beacon gives twice the gift.

## Days and nights

A day passes in about forty minutes. By day the waste is quiet: a stray or a pair now and then (every three or four minutes), and on the road about one thing in three ahead of you is a fight; the rest are camps, the fallen and cairns. After a fight you get a breather: nothing new comes for 90 seconds after the last creature near you falls (two minutes in the near lands).

At night the creatures rise in **rounds**. The next round comes 30 to 45 seconds after the last is cleared (or after 90 seconds, so stragglers can't hold it off). There are up to six rounds a night, each a little bigger, and the one before dawn is the largest. Night creatures have a quarter more health and strike a fifth harder, and more of them are heavies and shooters. Each night is a little worse than the last.

Light keeps them off: no creature rises within 40 paces of a burning campfire, or 15 of a torch. **Settlements are always safe**: nothing rises inside them or near their walls, nothing follows you in, and nothing outside can shoot you while you're inside.

When you fall, you wake knowing a little more: the nearest lair or ruin you had not found is marked on your map.

Sleep through the night by resting at a fire or a bedroll. In company, everyone must agree to sleep.

## Always a next goal

- **Smoke.** Red smoke rises over dead beacons, and dark smoke over lairs that are waiting. You can see both from far off.
- **What now.** The top of the journal (**J**) names three goals: the next step on the road, something near you, and a way to grow.
- **Guiding embers.** Now and then, embers drift toward the goal you are tracking. Turn them off in **Settings**.

## The map

Press **M**. Scroll to zoom, drag to move, right click to mark a spot. Marks are shared with your fellowship and show on the compass.

The whole map, sea and isles too, is black until you have been there. A lit beacon shows the land for about 500 metres around it, also for beacons lit in older worlds. Maps from older saves carry over.

## Roaming events

Every five to nine minutes something happens somewhere near you, marked on the compass and in What now:

- **A caravan under attack.** Drive off the attackers.
- **A fallen star.** Something bright fell out of the sky.
- **A supply cache.** Get to it before others do.
- **A box cat swarm.** A screaming pack gathers in the ruins.

Each one pays everyone near when it is done. Leave one alone and it may dig in as a **raid camp**: more foes, a strong one at its heart, and a bigger reward. A raid camp stays for twenty minutes or until you break it.

## Named nights

Some nights have a name. Hold until dawn without falling and the morning pays.

- **The Screaming Night:** box cats run in packs.
- **The Long Dark:** more forms walk than any night before.
- **The Hunter's Moon:** greater forms walk among the rest.
- **A Quiet Night:** few walk. Rest, or build.

The night eases after your fellowship falls, and grows as beacons are lit. Choose how hard nights press with **Night pace** in the world settings.

## Bestiary

The first time each kind of form falls to you, its page opens: what it is and what it fears. See [[Creatures]].

## Food and rest

Two small bars at the bottom left. Eat to keep **Food** up and rest to keep **Rest** up. When either runs low you heal slower and tire faster. Cooked food (at a cooking station) is far better than raw.

**Meals.** Cooked dishes are meals: you can have up to three at once, each adding health, stamina or healing for a while (shown at the bottom left). The same meal can be eaten again once it wears thin.

**Rested.** Sleep somewhere comfortable and you wake rested, earning more experience for a while. A fire, a bed, a roof, walls, a light and furniture all add comfort; an inn bed is the best of all.

## The lands

The lands grow harder the further you go: Ash first, then the Dunes and Forest, then the Salt and Marsh, and the Basalt last. Foes there have more health and hit harder. Each land has its own hazard, and a ward made at a workbench against it: a Dust Veil for the dunes' heat, a Spore Mask for the forest, Salt Goggles for the glare, Tar Boots for the marsh, an Ember Cloak for the basalt. Each also yields its own material (red iron, heartwood, salt crystal, tar, obsidian), which the better gun parts need.

## Towns and inns

Each town has houses, roads and an inn. At the inn you can buy a meal, rent a bed for the night, or listen for rumours that mark a ruin on your map.

## Gathering, making and building

Gather from trees, rocks, salt flats and heaps of ruin. Make things in your pack under **Make**; some need a workbench or a cooking station nearby. See [[Items]], [[Arms and Clothing|Arms-and-Clothing]], [[Making]] and [[Building]].

Gathering throws chips and the rock or tree shivers with each blow; the last blow is heavier.

Moving things: drag them between a chest and your pack, and choose how much a click moves (all, half, 10 or 1).

A **campfire** makes a base. Inside a base, every chest counts as part of your pack when you make or build, so a well-stocked base saves a lot of walking. Name your chests by opening them: the name shows above the chest.

## Settlements and work

People live in the settlements. They trade, they talk, and they give work. Work earns salt, standing with that settlement, and experience. Greater standing opens better goods. See [[Places]].

## The beacons

Nine beacons stand dead across the waste. Kindling them is the main story, and each one lit pushes the night back a little. Beacon oil is made at a workbench once you have learned how. While you work through the beacons, the quest line shows the next small step: get lamp oil, pick ash-bloom, make the oil, or carry it to the nearest dark beacon.

Each beacon lit gives a gift, lets you choose one boon out of three, and shows where the next dark beacon is. Dead beacons send up red smoke you can see from far off.

## Riding cats

Great stray cats roam near the towns by day. They shy away from running feet, so walk up softly. Feed one meat three times and it is yours. Press Interact to ride it; hold Sprint to run it until it is out of breath. Hold Interact on it to name it and give it barding (hide, bone or iron), which turns aside part of the harm it takes. Your cat walks faster than you can run. Harm while riding is shared with your cat; hurt too much, it throws you and must rest (or feed it meat). It waits where you leave it, and comes when you whistle (N).

## Falling

When your health runs out you fall, unconscious, with a timer. A friend can raise you by holding the interact key on you: quicker and with more health if they carry smelling salts. Hold **jump** to give up and wake at your bed (or where you began). What you carried is left where you fell, glowing blue, marked on your map.

## Experience

Everything you do earns experience. Each level gives a point to spend on [[Skills]].

## Beacon credit

A lit beacon burns for the whole world, but its boon is personal. Those near it when it catches, and those who fought at its second front, get the full gift and choose a boon. With a second front, the vigil lasts until the front is held (at least four minutes). A failed vigil gives the oil back. Those elsewhere get half the gift and no boon; they can walk to the lit beacon later and **keep a vigil** there to take its boon and credit for themselves. When a beacon is kindled you also see the next dark beacon and, past it, the smoke of that land's great one, both marked on your map.

## Ember runs (two or more)

In company, anyone can take an **ember** from the foot of a lit beacon and carry it to a dark one. No beacon oil is needed and the vigil is half as long. The carrier cannot shoot and walks slower, and creatures keep coming for the ember, so the others must escort them. If the carrier falls, the ember goes out.

## The ash-radio

Make an **ash-radio** at a workbench (or buy one from a smith) and carry it. Every few minutes it picks up a call for help somewhere near you: a caravan, a cache, a fallen star. An hour before dusk it tells you what kind of night is coming.

## Playtest log

Each sitting in the waste writes a short log on your own computer, in the game's user folder under `playtest/`. It notes each goal reached (a beacon lit, a ruin found, a quest step done, a night lived through), the time since the last goal, and when you fell. Nothing is sent anywhere. Share a log when you report how a session felt.

## Safe roads

Every town knows how safe its roads are, from 0 to 100: **Dangerous**, **Uneasy**, **Safe** or **Peaceful**. You see it when you talk to its people.

- Within about 500 m of a town, each foe you slay makes its roads a little safer (more for big foes and champions). Putting down a band met on the road adds 5, and breaking a raid camp nearby adds 15.
- Each day the roads slip back by 3, and by 8 more for every raid camp standing near.
- A band met on the road and left alone for eight minutes digs in as a raid camp, and the town's roads suffer for it.
- The town's folk remember who kept their roads: every 10 points you earn there is one more step of standing, which brings better prices.
- Near a town with safe roads, fewer of the things met on the road are fights. Near a dangerous one, more.

### What the roads bring

| Roads | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Dangerous** (under 25) | The gates are barred at night (the watch opens to you if your standing is 2 or more), traders ask a quarter more, and the town sends a plea. |
| **Uneasy** (25 to 59) | As things are. |
| **Safe** (60 to 89) | Traders sell more: tempered steel, clockwork, rare fittings, rockets, the elixir. Trade caravans run between safe towns; save one when it is set upon (or rob it, and both towns hear of it). |
| **Peaceful** (90 and up) | The town's watch walks its roads and shoots what comes near. |

- **Bounties:** a town posts a bounty on the raid camp nearest it (shown when you talk to its people). Break it for coin and standing.
- **Honours:** at 5 standing you are a **Friend** of the town, and its inn's beds are free. At 8 it sends you a gift. At 12 you are its **Warden**.
- **Rivals:** a town left dangerous beside a safe neighbour grumbles at you, and your standing there slips.
- **The map** shows each town's roads as a ring of colour: red, amber, green or gold.

## Every fire opens something

Each beacon lit opens a new thing to do. These go by the most fires that ever burned in the world, so a fire that gutters out does not take them back.

| Fire | Opens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strays to tame and ride |
| 2 | Survey: stand on a high place and nearby ruins and lairs (within 600 paces) go on your map |
| 3 | Planter boxes |
| 4 | Waystones |

| 6 | The longboat (the Longboat-wright's Book) |

## Fouled bowls

After the turn, the Mouth fouls the dark bowls. Each wants a key from beyond the shore before beacon oil will take:

- **Sixth fire:** Deep Oil, made from lamp oil and the Bell-Wound's ichor.
- **Seventh fire:** the Fleet Log, from Quietglass.
- **Eighth fire:** a far great one's heart.
- **Ninth fire:** three far coals, one from a shrine-bowl on each Far Shore.

Corvin, Ione and Sigrun tell you which. A key cleans its bowl for good, and goes back to you if the vigil fails.

## Waystones

Waystones wake with the fourth fire. One of a pair must stand in a lit beacon's ground or in a town, and both must stand on one land. A far isle links to the waste only along a lit sea lane. People pass through; cargo, ore, keys, far materials, an ember or a cat do not. A waystone that cannot link shows why.

## After an ending

The world goes on after an ending.

- **Great ones walk abroad.** Some dawns a great one leaves its lair and roams its own land. A banner says where.
- **Feast days.** Every fifth day one town keeps a feast: food and salt for those who come, and trade at four fifths of the price.
- **Completion.** Each quarter of the bestiary and of the map you have known pays a reward, up to the whole of both.

## The Second Vow

The Second Vow is the harder road after the first ending.

- Lit fires gutter, down to a third over three days. Tend a fire with lamp oil to make it full again. A guttering fire keeps the night back less far.
- The forms of the Far Shores walk the waste.
- The seventh night brings the Far Host.
- Everything is harder; the Far Shores take the vow lighter (x1.15).

## The ending vote

The ending is chosen by a vote at the page. The most votes win, the host breaks a tie, and the hall waits a minute for those still on their way.
