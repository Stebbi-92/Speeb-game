Brelm alpha 0.20.1: sights and fixes

- Your own hands are hidden on the guns for now, until they hold every gun well.
- The SMG's rear aperture and front post line up on the crosshair.
- The revolver's barrel points where you aim (it tipped down), and its sights sit right.
- The Auto Pistol fires about 1,200 rounds a minute, like a Glock 18C.
- Attachments sit properly on the new guns: the revolver's sights, barrels and compensator, the Autoshotgun's stocks, and its sights on its top rail. Grips on the Belt Gun still count but are not drawn, since it has its own front grip.

Brelm alpha 0.20.0: hands and new bodies

- Hands on every gun: you now see your arms and hands hold each gun and follow its reloads, with magazines, shells, rockets and fuel bottles coming out. Side arms use a two-hand grip with both thumbs forward.
- New guns: the pistol is now a G17 with a moving slide; a new Autoshotgun and a full-auto SMG. The revolver is a 357 Python whose cylinder turns each shot and swings out to reload. The belt gun has its own fire, reload and idle motions.
- New bodies: zombies, skeleton warriors, liches and the Ash Weaver are now properly animated, and the tusker is a furred wild boar that walks, trots and runs.
- Cigarettes: light one from the pack and it burns while you work. Friends see it and its smoke.
- The gun draws back and tips up when you stand right against a wall, instead of going through it.
- Dead trees lose the floating red band and yellow box. Great ones keep their own heads instead of wearing a Speeb face. A red dot shows only when you aim down the sight.

Brelm alpha 0.19.0: settings, menus and a bug hunt

- Lighter towns: townsfolk and the still, solid parts of a town are drawn together, so a town sends about half as many things to be drawn.
- Settings: a Low graphics preset for slower machines (shorter shadows, no glow), and a Render scale (100% to 50%) that draws the world smaller and sharpens it back up.
- Settings for everyone: Sprint, Sneak and Aim can be set to press-to-switch instead of hold; menus and words can be drawn at 90% to 120%; subtitles for spoken lines; colour-blind friendly road safety colours on the map.
- Error reports: if the game hit an error or closed suddenly, the title asks whether to send a short report (only the error and the game version). Settings can set it to always send or never.
- Menus: one look for every list, switch, slider and text box; Settings in six pages (Display, Controls, Sound, Accessibility, Game, Playing together); brightness, controller look speed and a separate controller invert; every menu works with a controller, and B goes back. Display changes ask before they stay. Join by code comes first in Walk Together. Arms for the trials now sit under The Trials, and Appearance has its own place on the title.
- Map: land not yet seen is dark ash with a faint kilometre grid instead of black; a north mark and a scale; the first look opens close in on you.
- Press Reload with a full gun to look it over. A slain enemy falls the way the blow drove it, then sinks into the ash.
- Controller buttons can be changed in Settings, Controls. With a controller, prompts and the first-steps guide name its buttons instead of keys.
- The beacon bell is now a recorded bell, deeper and with a long ring.
- Hosts can send a player away: Pause, Players, Send away. They can't come back under that name until the host closes the game.
- Messages under the compass wait their turn instead of replacing each other, and things picked up together are counted on one line.
- Fixes: quitting no longer moves your cat and boats to the middle of the map; a damaged save is kept aside instead of being written over; satchels left lying far from anyone blow away after four days (never your own belongings, or a broken chest's goods); buying makings, making salve or tea and selling it back no longer makes salt; enemy updates in co-op fit in one network packet; a joiner's game can no longer send what only the host may say.
- First steps: the guide now says to hold E to gather (it said hit), a new pilgrim starts with two cloth for the first bandage, and ruins near the start are guarded only by the weakest enemies, like the land around them.

Brelm alpha 0.18.0: the polish

- Plain words on screen: prompts, menus and the HUD say kill, enemy and player. Sprint and Sneak are the new names of Hasten and Tread softly. The waste's own voice stays in lore, rumours and talk.
- Each system shows up when you first need it: fear at the first night, hardships at the first hard land, trades at the first town, road safety at the first plea, seasons from day five. One who joins later sees all the world has reached.
- Music: a small score of free (CC0) tracks for dawn, night, the seventh night, towns, the sea, fights and great ones, with a swell when a beacon catches. It has its own Music slider in Settings.
- Together: a friend's perfect guard now turns enemies' blows as yours does. One who joins sees the events under way. Two spending the same chest at the same moment no longer both get the goods: the host settles it.

Brelm alpha 0.17.0: the long road and the keeping

The road across the sea
- After the turn, the Mouth fouls the dark bowls. Each wants a key from beyond the shore before beacon oil will take: Deep Oil (lamp oil and the Bell-Wound's ichor) for the sixth fire, the Fleet Log from Quietglass for the seventh, a far great one's heart for the eighth, and three far coals for the ninth, one from a shrine-bowl on each Far Shore. Corvin, Ione and Sigrun tell you which. A key cleans its bowl for good, and goes back to you if the vigil fails.
- Danger 5 on the Far Shores after the turn: foes have x3.6 health and x2.1 harm, champions are twice as common, and kills teach more.
- Five far materials: ice iron, sunglass, steppe horn, lens glass and dome steel, gathered on their own land and found in its ruins.
- A fifth step of work on arms: Far-forged (x1.60). Far Plate: the most plate, warm, and no weight. Far Barding for the cat.
- Three far arms: the Sunglass Carbine (one fast, flat rifle round), the Dome-steel Hand Cannon (a two-shell side arm that staggers heavy forms) and the Ice-iron War-pick (through any plate, and it freezes lesser forms a moment). The far great ones leave the books that teach them.

Every fire opens something new
- 1 strays to tame and ride, 2 survey from high places (ruins and lairs go on your map), 3 planters, 4 waystones, 5 the sloop, 6 the longboat, 7 Discern (hidden and unseen forms on your compass), 8 Bulwark (the first blow of each night is taken), 9 the Ferryman's Way. These go by the most fires that ever burned in the world.
- Six boons are now things to do: Rallying Cry (your whistle gives those near their breath and a burst of speed), Second Relic (two spirit-cats at once), Lantern-bearer (no dread near you), Pack-Cat (+8 pack with a cat), Quick Camp (one free campfire a night) and Deep Diver (half the breath swimming).
- Waystones wake with the fourth fire. One of a pair must stand in a lit beacon's ground or a town, and both on one land; a far isle links to the waste only along a lit sea lane. People pass; cargo, ore, keys, far materials, an ember or a cat do not.

Great ones and the keeping
- Every great one leaves its own named fittings (rework a part into a Storied one with its name and trait), and the first time it falls, a book.
- The Bell-Wound's trophy has a power: every eighth blow that lands strikes twice as hard.
- The Trophy Hall: hang up to three trophies at a base; each gives a third of its power to all there. Never both: the shrine's power or the hall's, whichever is stronger.
- Renown: past level 30, experience fills renown. Each step gives a fresh start for your skills, the Barding of Renown, or another trophy place (up to five).
- After an ending the world goes on: great ones walk abroad in their own lands, every fifth day a town keeps a feast (food, salt and cheap trade), and each quarter of the bestiary and the map pays a reward.
- The Second Vow adds rules: lit fires gutter to a third over three days and are tended with lamp oil, the Far Shores' forms walk the waste, and the seventh night brings the Far Host. The Far Shores take the vow lighter (x1.15).

Playing together
- A beacon's boon and gift go to everyone near when it caught and everyone who fought at its second front. With a second front the vigil lasts until the front is held (at least four minutes). A failed vigil gives the oil back.
- The ending is chosen by a vote at the page: the most votes win, the host breaks a tie, and the hall waits a minute for those on their way.
- Anyone can make tempered steel and clockwork, at three times the cost; the Smith and the Tinker make it cheaply.

Controls
- One key per action: Interact is E, best healing is F.
- Controller: hold Back and point a stick for the quick wheel: healing, whistle, smelling salts, fellowship and the relics.

Also
- The cat's saddle (by YaredGmGm, CC-BY) now faces forward and sits under the rider.
- Breaking change: worlds and characters from 0.16 may not carry over cleanly.

Brelm alpha 0.16.0: the far lands

- The fifth beacon is the turn: the sea opens. The whirlpool and the far isles show on the horizon, the seventh night's raid comes up out of the water (the Drowned Tide), and champions rise in every land past the first ring.
- The far sea: Quietglass, the city of glass, and the Thousand Domes, the bunker coast.
- Past it, the Far Shores, each about 2 km across: the Frostmark (snow, lava and steam), the Sunward Reach (red mesas and dry canyons) and the Pale Steppe (grey grass and slow rivers).
- Each far land has its own foes, the thirteen far forms among them, and ruins about every 300 m.
- New hardships with their wards: cold on the Frostmark (Frostfolk Sheepskin), thirst on the Sunward Reach by day (Water Gourd), and getting lost on the Pale Steppe (Rider's Bone Compass).
- Three far towns: Hearthholm, the Hanging Town and Stillwater, each with two new people.
- Five far great ones, each with its own trophy and power: the Archivist, the Lantern-Breaker, the Glacier Mother, the Dry River and the Spine-King.
- The Lighthouse chain: ten dark lighthouses on the far sea. Pour lamp oil and hold the rock to light one. Light both on a way and it becomes a safe sea lane, shown on the map.
- The Kelp Forest: boats go slowly, and weed eels pull swimmers down.
- The map reaches the whole far sea. Scroll to zoom in further.

Fixes from a full sweep of the game:
- Leaving the game while the death countdown runs no longer keeps both your pack and the satchel you dropped.
- Dying on a boat no longer puts you back on it.
- Drowning no longer breaks your armour.
- Friends can always raise a fallen player.
- A refused join now says why (wrong version, wrong word, full).
- Old saves keep their gathered spots and ammo piles. Worlds with five or more beacons already lit get the turn.
- New characters joining a fellowship no longer get credit for beacons they never lit.
- Searched crates keep what was stored in them when they fill again.
- Two errands that both want a keepsake no longer lock each other.
- Settlers remember what they asked for after a reload.
- Sell all trinkets sells every stack.
- Vault keepers: a fallen friend climbs out with the rest and takes a share.
- The tar and white powers now work.
- Refused builds, lighthouse lamps and beacon oil give back what was paid.
- Town road safety and gifts agree for everyone in the fellowship.
- Militia shoot on dedicated servers.
- Far Shore trees no longer vanish while you stand among them. Nothing grows in the far towns or on lairs.
- The Pale Steppe landing sits at the sea, not on a river.

Brelm alpha 0.15.0: the living waste

- Each town knows how safe its roads are: Dangerous, Uneasy, Safe or Peaceful. Slay foes near it, put down the bands on its roads and break raid camps nearby, and its roads grow safer. Run from them and leave camps standing, and they grow worse day by day.
- Townsfolk remember who kept their roads: every ten points you earn there is a step of standing, and better prices.
- Safe roads bring fewer fights near the town; dangerous roads bring more.
- A town posts a bounty on the raid camp nearest it. Break it for coin and standing.
- At Safe roads, traders sell more (tempered steel, clockwork, rare fittings and more), and trade caravans run between safe towns. Save one set upon, or rob it, and both towns hear of it.
- At Dangerous roads, a town bars its gates at night and its traders ask a quarter more. Knock, and the watch opens only to those it knows.
- At Peaceful roads, the town's watch walks its roads and shoots what comes near.
- When a town's roads turn dangerous, it sends you a plea, and What now points you to it.
- Honours: at 5 standing you are a Friend of the town and its inn's beds are free; at 8 it sends a gift; at 12 you are its Warden.
- A town left dangerous beside a safe neighbour grumbles at you.
- The map shows each town's roads as a ring of colour.
- Fixed: a town's ammunition in the trade lists was priced ten times over.
- A band met on the road and left alone digs in as a raid camp.
- The seventh night's raid changes as the lands are lit: the Hollow Pilgrimage, then the Unlit War Band, then the Iron Host, each with its own lead.
- Settlers teach the fellowship a recipe of their trade, and ask for goods: leave them in the base's chest, and they pay at dawn.
- The day before a season turns, you are told what it brings.

Brelm alpha 0.14.0: walk together

- Snow, ash and rain are soft round flecks that vanish close to your eye, so none fill the view.
- The puffs a far form gives before its way are soft and round too.
- A red dot sight's dot no longer shows through walls and trees on another pilgrim's gun.
- Town roofs sit the right way: the ridge runs along the long side of the house.
- Built doors fill their doorway.
- The pack's tabs work again: a faded revelation card had sat over them and taken their clicks.
- T uses the best healing you carry (the elixir first if you burn or are in tar).
- The great ones have bodies of their own, each with its Speeb's head: a hound of embers, a sand worm, a stone golem, a long bone beast and a tar-black widow.
- The dead and the Unlit have true bodies: a rotting walker, a black knight, a horned lich, a sheet ghost, a bare skeleton and a soldier in black.
- What lies about the waste is real now: rusted barrels, ammo crates, sacks, jerrycans, tyres, stumps, roots and boulders in place of the toy-like pieces.
- No two towns are laid out alike: some crowd to one side with a fenced yard on the other, some stand in squared rows or along a crooked lane, some in two knots. Their walls run in and out.
- Farmsteads stand outside the towns' walls, and lone barns out in the waste, seen from far off.
- The drifting forms fly on bat wings. Kulshedra, the she-dragon of Kalaja, has a dragon's body.
- Hares, goats and tuskers have real bodies, and the raft is a raft of lashed logs.
- Living trees stand in clumps through the dead forest and on the isles. You cannot walk through their trunks.
- Air support in the trials flies real aircraft: a gunship, a ground-attack jet and fighters.
- The lesser forms have real cat bodies now, that stand, walk and stalk.
- Riding cats are great sphynx cats now, with a Speeb's head, that walk, trot and gallop.
- Mounts look ahead, and turn their heads toward you only as far as a neck goes.
- Drifting motes are only in the marsh now.
- Pictures of the waste behind the loading screen.
- Names float above the foes, never inside their bodies, and plate sits on their chests.
- Foes no longer grow stronger when your guns do. They are as strong as their land: a worked-up gun is felt.
- Guns and blades are worked up in steps of +15%. Blades can be worked up now too. Better arms cost more to work.
- Pistol 18, Shotgun pellets 12, Machine Gun 17, Hatchet 42, Obsidian Glaive 120. Ash Stalkers take a little more.
- Each lit beacon adds 10 most health, up to +60.
- Great ones grow less with each extra player.
- In a fellowship, a blade blow freezes the struck foe for a blink and jolts your view.
- After a fight by day you get a breather. Strays come less often, and fewer road events are fights.
- Nights come in rounds, with a breath between, the largest before dawn. Night foes are tougher.
- Settlements are always safe: nothing outside can shoot you while you are inside.
- The Ashen Knight carries his own flamberge, and his armour is his plate. The Unlit Breaker swings a real sledgehammer.
- Gathering lands: a swing, then the blow, with its own sound for wood, stone, iron, leaf and bone. What you take rises off the node, and the last blow fells a tree or breaks a rock apart.
- Poise: every blow wears a foe's footing, blades and heavy blows most. Broken, it reels and takes more harm.
- A perfect guard, raised just as a blow lands, costs nothing and leaves the foe reeling.
- Gunmen fire rounds you can see. Keep moving, or dodge as one comes.
- Heavy foes sweep, lunge and grab, each with its own warning.
- Foes come in mixed packs, and champions walk the far lands.
- Slain foes often leave a few rounds, more in the harder lands. Never healing.
- Ammunition is balanced to what it does: a bench makes 24 shells, 60 pistol rounds or 80 rifle rounds. Traders sell shells at 12 salt for ten, rounds at 6, and buy them back for less than they cost to make.
- Walking together: pilgrims who keep together fall into squads of two to four. What one slays, the others near learn from.
- Loot from great ones and road events falls as a satchel for each player, seen only by its owner.
- A friend who joins a fellowship already on the road comes with better kit, more for each beacon lit.
- What now names a friend to ask for what only their trade makes.
- The vault keeper's gate must be wound up at its two winches: alone it is slow, two together quick. The way into a vault's first room is no longer shut.
- Boats have stations: helm, sail, lookout, gun and oars. A sail-hand runs the boat faster, the lookout calls what moves on the water, the gunner hits harder, and each oar rows faster. Hold interact on the boat to change station.
- With five or more, kindling a beacon also brings a raid on your nearest camp: a second front.

Brelm alpha 0.13.0: Feel

The map
- The map starts black. You see only the land you have walked, and a lit beacon shows the land for about 500 m around it.

Hits and foes
- Struck forms rock back from the blow. Hits sound like what they strike: flesh, stone, bone or plate.
- Far forms show a sign before they use their ways, so you can see a charge or a volley coming.

Arms on the road
- In the waste you carry two long guns, a side arm and a blade. Change what you carry at camp, in a town or at a workbench. Keys 1 to 3 are the guns, 4 the blade, 5 to 9 the relics.
- The shotgun loads real shells one at a time and throws out the spent ones.
- The belt gun's and the sniper rifle's iron sights now line up.
- New muzzle flashes, set at each gun's muzzle.
- Sights clamp onto the rail with no riser. The Red Dot and Reflex have a small, bright dot, and the Holo a thin ring round a dot.
- The Short Scope is a real scope now: you look through it at 4x. The Scope and the sniper rifle's glass are 8x, and the sniper's iron sights 2x.
- Fixed parts that floated off the gun: muzzle parts and long barrels sit on the muzzle, grips sit under the fore-end, and stocks and receiver plates sit on the gun. A side arm's heavy barrel no longer covers the slide.

Sound
- Gunshots are louder and the flamethrower is much quieter.
- Bites, roars, growls, shrieks, footsteps, blows, the pump and shells now use real recordings.

The world
- The ground loads in tiles, in full detail near you and coarse far away.
- The loading screen shows a progress bar and hints that change.
- The light over a lit beacon and the red smoke over a dead one are softer, with wisps rising up them.
